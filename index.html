About me

I am a Senior Data Scientist at CVS Health, working in Caremark's Pricing and Product Analytics team. I build the analysis behind pricing decisions for generic pharmacy drugs: comparing pricing scenarios, evaluating financial outcomes and member savings, and translating the results for business stakeholders.

My path into data science started in engineering. I worked as an aerospace engineer at the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), then earned a PhD in Industrial and Enterprise Systems Engineering at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. My doctoral research used neural networks to build machine learning models for simulating graphene and other 2D materials, published in Computational Materials Science and at AIAA SciTech and ASME conferences. Before CVS Health, I was a Senior Data Scientist at ComEd, applying data science to the utility industry.

That mix taught me to treat every model as a system. The inputs, assumptions, costs and the people affected matter as much as the math, and a recommendation is only useful if it holds up in practice. I am now completing a Master of Science in Artificial Intelligence at Indiana Wesleyan University to deepen my machine learning practice and to lead responsible AI adoption in healthcare analytics.

My goal is to lead AI initiatives that are reliable enough for high-stakes decisions and clear enough that the people who rely on them understand their limits.

Personal value proposition

I help healthcare and analytics teams turn complex, high-stakes data into AI and pricing decisions they can trust.

Drawing on a PhD in systems engineering and experience across aerospace, energy and pharmacy pricing, I build models with validated inputs, documented assumptions and clear guardrails. Then I explain them in plain language, so stakeholders know what a model can do, where it stops and how to act on it.

Research

Peer-reviewed work from my doctoral research at the University of Illinois, completed before this program. It shows my hands-on experience with neural networks, simulation and collaborative research, and it is separate from my course projects.

  • Journal article: Computational Materials Science, 2023

    Reliable machine learning potentials for graphene

    A neural network trained on quantum mechanical calculations that predicts the energy of carbon atoms in graphene with near-quantum accuracy, making simulations of more than 12,000 atoms practical. First-authored, peer reviewed and cited more than 40 times.

    Read the summary Read the free preprint
  • Journal article: The Journal of Physical Chemistry C, 2024

    Simulating how a 2D material templates plastic crystals

    An all-atom molecular dynamics study showing that polyethylene crystallizes outward from a MoSe2 surface and that the surface sets the direction of the chains, explained by a balance of entropy, adhesion and surface texture. First-authored with Oak Ridge National Laboratory and Villanova University.

    Read the summary View the article
  • Journal article: Macromolecules, 2022

    Atomic edges that guide how plastics crystallize

    A study with Oak Ridge National Laboratory and Villanova University combining experiments, quantum calculations and molecular dynamics. It shows that the atomic edges of a two-dimensional material steer how polyethylene chains align as they crystallize. Third of eight authors.
